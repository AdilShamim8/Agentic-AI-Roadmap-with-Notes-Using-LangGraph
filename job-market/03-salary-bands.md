See [field guide chapter 7](../field-guide/07-salary-and-career-trajectory.md) for the full salary data, including bands by level, region, and company stage.

## Summary (US, 2026)

| Level | Years | Base | Total comp |
|-------|-------|------|------------|
| Junior AI Engineer | 0-2 | $130K-$170K | $140K-$200K |
| AI Engineer | 2-4 | $170K-$220K | $200K-$300K |
| Senior AI Engineer | 4-7 | $220K-$280K | $300K-$450K |
| Staff AI Engineer | 7-10 | $280K-$350K | $450K-$700K |
| Principal AI Engineer | 10+ | $350K-$450K | $700K-$1M+ |

Agentic AI engineer roles command a 10-20% premium over broader AI engineer roles.

## Where to verify

- [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/) - filter by "AI Engineer". Self-reported, but the largest dataset.
- [builtin.com](https://builtin.com/) - job postings with salary ranges. Required by law in many US states.
- [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/) - self-reported, less reliable than Levels.fyi.

## Negotiation

- Always negotiate. The first offer is rarely the best offer.
- Have a competing offer. It is the strongest leverage.
- Negotiate equity, not just base. At a startup, equity is the upside.
- Use Levels.fyi data to anchor. "I have seen comp for this role at $X on Levels.fyi."
- Be ready to walk. If the company will not negotiate, decide whether the role is worth the first offer.
