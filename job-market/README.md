## The headline numbers

- 70% of "AI engineer" roles now include agentic components (up from 40% at the start of 2026).
- 35.9% of AI-first roles mention RAG.
- LangGraph is mentioned in 8.0% of AI engineer postings and 35% of agentic AI engineer postings.
- MCP is mentioned in 18% of agentic AI engineer postings (up from 2% in January 2026).
- 95.6% of roles are applied/production, not research.

See [01 2026 skills analysis](01-2026-skills-analysis.md) for the full breakdown.

## Where to find jobs

- [builtin.com](https://builtin.com/) - the primary source for this analysis. Search "AI engineer" or "agentic AI engineer".
- [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/) - broader, but more noise.
- [Hugging Face jobs](https://huggingface.co/jobs) - AI-specific.
- [AI Engineer Jobs](https://aiengineer.jobs/) - aggregator.
- Company career pages directly. The companies in [field guide chapter 3](../field-guide/03-job-archetypes.md) are hiring.

## How to read a job posting

An "AI engineer" posting might be:

- An agentic AI engineer role (builds agents). Look for: LangGraph, multi-agent, evaluation, MCP.
- A RAG-focused role (builds retrieval systems). Look for: RAG, vector databases, embeddings.
- A prompt engineering role (writes prompts). Look for: prompt engineering, A/B testing, no orchestration framework.
- A rebranded ML role (trains models). Look for: PyTorch, TensorFlow, training, fine-tuning.

Read the responsibilities, not the title. The title is unreliable.
