# Field guide

The field guide covers the role, the skills, the job market, the interview process, and career trajectory for agentic AI engineers in 2026. Read it alongside the curriculum - the curriculum teaches you what to build, the field guide teaches you what to do with what you build.

This guide is modeled on Alexey Grigorev's [AI Engineering Field Guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide), which is the benchmark for this kind of resource. Where Grigorev's guide covers AI engineering broadly, this one focuses on the agentic subset.

## Chapters

- [01 The agentic AI engineer role](01-the-agentic-ai-engineer-role.md) - what the role is, how it differs from ML engineering and data science
- [02 Skills that matter in 2026](02-skills-that-matter.md) - the skill stack, what is baseline, what differentiates
- [03 Job archetypes](03-job-archetypes.md) - AI-First, AI-Support, ML, and where agentic AI fits
- [04 A day in the life](04-day-in-the-life.md) - what the work actually looks like
- [05 Interview prep](05-interview-prep.md) - system design for agents, coding rounds, behavioral
- [06 Take-home assignments](06-take-home-assignments.md) - what companies assign and how to approach it
- [07 Salary and career trajectory](07-salary-and-career-trajectory.md) - what agentic AI engineers earn, how the career grows

## How to use this guide

Read chapter 1 to understand the role. Read chapter 2 to identify your skill gaps (and use the [learning paths](../learning-paths/) to fill them). Read chapters 3-4 to set expectations about the job. Read chapters 5-7 when you are ready to apply.

## The principle

The field guide is data-anchored. Every claim about the job market, salaries, or interview processes is sourced - to job postings, to survey data, or to practitioner reports. Unsourced claims are not included. If you find an unsourced claim, open an issue.
